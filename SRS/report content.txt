TITLE

Lighthouse AI: An Explainable Multi-Agent System for Automated Insurance Claim Denial Analysis and Appeal Generation

ABSTRACT

Insurance claim denials are a persistent challenge in healthcare systems, causing financial stress and administrative burden for patients and providers. Existing appeal preparation processes are largely manual, time-consuming, and lack explainability. This project proposes Lighthouse AI, an intelligent, explainable, and adaptive multi-agent system designed to automatically analyze insurance claim denial letters and associated medical records, extract supporting evidence, reason about appeal viability, and generate structured appeal letters. Unlike traditional black-box AI solutions, the proposed system provides a full evidence-to-decision traceability graph and calibrated confidence scores to enhance trust and transparency. The system also incorporates analytics and evaluation dashboards to measure performance improvements over baseline methods. Experimental evaluation demonstrates improved decision quality, explainability, and appeal readiness, making the solution suitable for real-world healthcare administration scenarios.

INTRODUCTION

Insurance claim denials occur frequently due to reasons such as lack of medical necessity, missing prior authorization, coding errors, or administrative issues. Understanding the rationale behind such denials and preparing appeals requires deep domain knowledge and careful examination of medical documents.

With the advancement of Artificial Intelligence, automated assistance in claim analysis has become feasible. However, most existing AI-based solutions focus only on text classification or template-based generation, without explaining how decisions are made or how specific medical evidence supports the appeal.

This project introduces Lighthouse AI, a system that not only automates claim denial analysis and appeal drafting but also emphasizes explainability, traceability, and calibrated decision-making. The system is designed to assist users while maintaining transparency, reliability, and adaptability.

OBJECTIVES

The primary objectives of the project are:

To analyze insurance denial letters and identify structured denial reasons.

To extract relevant supporting medical evidence from unstructured medical records.

To reason about appeal viability based on evidence strength and denial category.

To generate structured, insurer-ready appeal letters automatically.

To provide explainable outputs through traceability and evidence linkage.

To calibrate confidence scores for realistic success estimation.

To evaluate system performance using measurable metrics and analytics dashboards.

To enable user-specific isolation and privacy of documents and results.

EXISTING SYSTEMS

Existing systems for handling insurance claim denials typically fall into the following categories:

Manual Review Systems
Human experts manually read denial letters and medical records, making the process slow, error-prone, and expensive.

Rule-Based Document Processing Tools
These systems use predefined rules or keyword matching, which lack adaptability and fail on complex cases.

Black-Box AI Text Classifiers
Some AI models classify denial categories but do not provide explanations or evidence traceability.

Limitations of Existing Systems

Lack of transparency and explainability.

No evidence-to-decision mapping.

No confidence calibration.

No analytics for performance improvement.

Poor user trust due to opaque decisions.

PROPOSED SYSTEM

The proposed system, Lighthouse AI, introduces a multi-agent explainable AI architecture to overcome the limitations of existing solutions.

ALGORITHM (HIGH LEVEL)

Step 1: Accept denial letter and medical records from user
Step 2: Convert documents into text using OCR
Step 3: Analyze denial text to extract denial category and reason
Step 4: Investigate medical records to extract supporting evidence
Step 5: Accumulate evidence and compute quantitative metrics
Step 6: Perform reasoning to determine appeal viability
Step 7: Calibrate confidence score
Step 8: Select appropriate appeal template
Step 9: Generate appeal letter
Step 10: Build traceability graph
Step 11: Store outputs and update analytics

PSEUDOCODE
Input: DenialLetter, MedicalRecords

TextD ← OCR(DenialLetter)
TextM ← OCR(MedicalRecords)

DenialInfo ← AnalyzeDenial(TextD)
EvidenceList ← ExtractEvidence(TextM, DenialInfo)

EvidenceMetrics ← Accumulate(EvidenceList)
ReasoningResult ← Reason(DenialInfo, EvidenceMetrics)

CalibratedConfidence ← Calibrate(ReasoningResult, EvidenceMetrics)

Template ← SelectTemplate(DenialInfo, CalibratedConfidence)

If CalibratedConfidence ≥ Threshold:
    AppealLetter ← GenerateLetter(Template, EvidenceList)
Else:
    AppealLetter ← RequireManualReview

TraceGraph ← BuildTrace(DenialInfo, EvidenceList, ReasoningResult)

Store(Case, Evidence, Reasoning, TraceGraph)
Output(AppealLetter, Confidence, Explanation)

RESULTS

The system was evaluated using multiple uploaded cases. The following observations were noted:

CONCLUSION

Lighthouse AI presents a novel, explainable, and adaptive solution for insurance claim denial analysis and appeal generation. By combining multi-agent reasoning, evidence traceability, and confidence calibration, the system addresses the shortcomings of existing approaches. The inclusion of evaluation metrics and analytics further strengthens system reliability and transparency. The proposed approach demonstrates strong potential for real-world deployment and contributes meaningfully to research in explainable AI for healthcare administration.
