To the Appeals Committee:

I am writing on behalf of my client, Mr. John Doe, to formally appeal the denial of coverage for the medically necessary left knee arthroscopy performed on October 14, 2025. The denial was based on the assertion that the patient failed to attempt 12 weeks of conservative therapy.

This denial is invalid, clinically unsound, and factually incorrect based on the following three points:

1. "Locked Knee" Exception Met (Policy # P-2024-KNEE Subsection C)
Your own clinical guidelines state that the 12-week conservative therapy requirement is waived if there is evidence of a "locked knee" or mechanical block to motion.

Evidence: Please review the attached clinical notes from Dr. Vance dated 08/22/2025 and 09/15/2025. Both clearly document "mechanical locking," "catching," and an "inability to fully extend the knee."

Visual Proof: The MRI report (Attached as Exhibit A) describes a "displaced bucket-handle tear flipping into the intercondylar notch."

Argument: Physical therapy cannot repair a displaced tissue fragment that is physically blocking the hinge mechanism of the knee. Surgery was not elective; it was urgent to restore joint function.

2. Incorrect Calculation of Conservative Treatment Timeline
Even if the locking exception is ignored, your calculation of conservative therapy is erroneous.

Mr. Doe began documented home exercise programs and NSAID management prescribed by his Primary Care Physician, Dr. Richards, on June 1, 2025 (Notes attached as Exhibit B).

Formal Physical Therapy began August 1, 2025.

The total duration of conservative management from June 1 to the surgery date of October 14 exceeds 18 weeks, well beyond your 12-week requirement.

3. Prevention of Further Harm (Standard of Care)
According to the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) guidelines, immediate arthroscopy is the standard of care for symptomatic meniscal tears causing mechanical blocking. Delaying surgery for arbitrary administrative timelines puts the patient at risk for further permanent cartilage damage. The Operative Report confirms this damage ("Grade III chondromalacia") had already begun due to the mechanical rubbing of the tear against the femoral condyle.

Conclusion:
The surgery was medically necessary, met your policy's written exceptions for mechanical symptoms, and the patient actually exceeded the conservative care timeline when PCP management is included.
